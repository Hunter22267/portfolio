**Topic:** The Water Cycle

### Overview
The water cycle is the continuous movement of water on, above, and below Earth's surface. Water moves between the atmosphere, land, oceans, and underground storage. The cycle has no fixed starting or ending point.

### What Drives the Water Cycle?
- **Solar energy:** Energy from the Sun causes liquid water to evaporate.
- **Gravity:** Gravity pulls precipitation toward Earth's surface and moves water downhill through runoff and groundwater flow.

### Key Processes

#### 1. Evaporation
Evaporation occurs when liquid water changes into water vapor. It happens at the surfaces of oceans, lakes, rivers, and soil. Solar energy provides much of the energy needed for this process.

#### 2. Transpiration
Transpiration is the release of water vapor from plant leaves. Plants absorb water through their roots, transport it through their tissues, and release some of it into the atmosphere. Evaporation and transpiration together are called evapotranspiration.

#### 3. Condensation
Condensation occurs when water vapor cools and changes into tiny liquid water droplets or ice crystals. These droplets and crystals contribute to cloud formation.

#### 4. Precipitation
Precipitation occurs when water falls from clouds to Earth's surface. Common forms include rain, snow, sleet, and hail.

#### 5. Runoff and Infiltration
- **Runoff:** Water flows across the land surface into streams, rivers, lakes, or oceans.
- **Infiltration:** Water soaks into the ground. Some of this water replenishes groundwater storage.

#### 6. Groundwater Flow
Groundwater moves through soil and rock below Earth's surface. It can eventually return to the surface through springs or discharge into rivers, lakes, and oceans.

#### 7. Snow and Ice Storage
Water can remain frozen in snowfields, glaciers, and ice caps. When snow and ice melt, the resulting water can enter streams, flow across land, or infiltrate the ground.

### How the Processes Connect
1. Solar energy causes water to evaporate from Earth's surface.
2. Water vapor enters the atmosphere and may cool and condense, contributing to cloud formation.
3. Water returns to Earth's surface through precipitation.
4. Precipitation may become runoff, infiltrate the ground, accumulate as snow or ice, or return directly to bodies of water.
5. Water continues moving through surface water, groundwater, and the atmosphere.

### Key Takeaways
- The water cycle continuously moves water between Earth's surface, underground storage, and the atmosphere.
- Solar energy and gravity are the main natural forces driving the cycle.
- Water can change between liquid, solid, and gaseous states.
- Water follows different paths through the cycle; it does not always pass through every process in the same order.
